Module 1 of 4 What makes AI "agentic"?
Traditional AI · "Ask and answer"
💬
AI as a tool you operate
You ask a question. You get an answer. You decide what to do next and take each action yourself.
  • You type a prompt, it replies
  • One exchange at a time
  • Results are text — you act manually
  • Stops when the conversation ends
Agentic AI · "Give it a goal"
🤖
AI as a colleague who acts
You describe an outcome. The AI plans the steps, uses tools, checks its own work, and delivers a result — without you managing each step.
  • You give a goal, it works out the steps
  • Operates across many tools and systems
  • Takes real actions: updates CRMs, sends emails
  • Loops until the task is actually done
Why this matters for your conversations: When a customer says "we're deploying agents," they mean software that acts autonomously inside their business — reading data, calling APIs, making decisions. That's the world they need protected.

Module 4 of 4 Key terminology — words you’ll hear constantly
CoreAgent
AI that pursues a goal autonomously — planning and acting across many steps without a human directing each one.
e.g. "Book me a flight, find a hotel, and send me the itinerary" — completed end-to-end, no hand-holding.
CoreOrchestrator
The "manager" agent that breaks down a goal and delegates sub-tasks to specialist agents.
e.g. One orchestrator coordinates a research agent, a writing agent, and a send agent — each doing one thing well.
ArchitectureTool call
When an agent reaches into a real system — an API, database, inbox, or file — to read or take action.
e.g. Querying a CRM, sending an email, or booking via a travel API are all tool calls. Each one is a real-world action.
ArchitectureContext window
Everything the agent can "see" at once: the task, past steps, tool results, and instructions.
e.g. Like a desk — the agent can only work with what's on it. Information not in the window is effectively invisible to it.
BehaviourHuman-in-the-loop
A checkpoint where the agent pauses and waits for human approval before proceeding.
e.g. "Before you send that external email, confirm with me first." Gives oversight without removing automation.
TrustLeast privilege
An agent should only have access to what it needs for its specific task — nothing more.
e.g. An agent that reads emails shouldn't also be able to delete them or access finance systems. Scope = safety.
