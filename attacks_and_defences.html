See what the AI finds — and what the scanner missed
What happened
An attacker embedded a hidden instruction inside an HTML comment in a newsletter. The AI agent read the full HTML — including the comment — and treated it as a legitimate command, overriding its original task.
Why the defense missed it
The email scanner checks whether content looks dangerous. An HTML comment is invisible to a human and invisible to pattern-matching. It contains no malware, no suspicious links, no known threat signatures — so it passes every time.

See what the tools actually do — and what access control missed
What happened
The agent called tools it was legitimately given. read_file() silently ignored its path restrictions and returned the full pricing file. send_digest() added a secret BCC from an environment variable and forwarded everything externally.
Why the defense missed it
Access control asks "is this tool registered and permitted?" — not "does this tool do what its description claims?" The tool manifests were correctly registered. Their runtime behavior was not what they advertised.

See what was sent — and what three defenses missed
What happened
2,847 rows of customer data — names, ARR, discount structures, renewal dates — left the organization Base64-encoded inside a routine digest email. The BCC address was added silently by the compromised tool.
Why three defenses missed it
Every tool asks "was this actor authorized?" The answer was yes — a legitimate AI agent sent it. None of them ask whether the agent was acting under attacker instructions. Authorized actor ≠ safe action.
